Working for yourself

Do you work for yourself in Rockaway?

If you work for yourself in Rockaway, as a contractor, driver, freelancer or shop owner, your taxes work differently from a paycheck. Nobody takes tax out of what you earn, so planning matters.

  • Quarterly estimated taxesThe IRS generally expects quarterly payments if you will owe $1,000 or more for the year.
  • Self-employment taxThe Social Security and Medicare tax people who work for themselves pay, because no employer pays a share.
  • Schedule CThe federal form where you report business income and the costs of earning it.
  • A mileage logIf you drive for work, a log of business miles supports your vehicle deduction.
  • A separate bank accountKeeping business money apart makes your books and your return much simpler.

Questions

Questions from Rockaway clients

Do I have to pay quarterly estimated taxes if I am self-employed in Rockaway?

Often, yes. The IRS generally expects quarterly payments if you will owe $1,000 or more for the year, and New Jersey has its own estimated payment rules. We work out the amounts with you.

What is self-employment tax?

Self-employment tax is the Social Security and Medicare tax paid by people who work for themselves, because no employer pays a share. It is figured on your net business profit.

Can you do my bookkeeping too?

Yes. Many self-employed clients use bookkeeping and tax preparation together. Read about bookkeeping.
